# Status

What is known about the FS1R, what is modelled, and what nobody knows. This file is the reference the engine is written against. It is not a task list, and it is not a history.

Three words carry every claim below.

- **KNOWN** is read from the firmware, its tables or the board. It needs no calibration and a disagreement with the hardware is a bug.
- **INFERRED** is modelled, because the two custom chips have no public documentation. Every inferred constant lives in one place, `src/fs1r/cal.h`, so calibrating against a recording is a table edit rather than a hunt through the engine.
- **UNKNOWN** is what no file, photo or recording tells us yet.

Moving a thing from INFERRED to KNOWN is the work. [docs/findings.md](docs/findings.md) records each move and the evidence that made it. [docs/fidelity_plan.md](docs/fidelity_plan.md) ranks what is still open by how far off it is.

## Where the numbers stand

Against rgwan's digital recording of the built-in demo, which the unit plays from its own EPROM so the same byte stream drives both, with the effects in the path on both sides (the Data List models of 0.5.2): envelope correlation median 0.976, worst 0.908. Mean band error 4.92 dB, tilt 1.96 dB, and per band from 40 Hz to 10 kHz, 2.8, 2.1, 1.6, 1.8, 0.7, 1.0, 1.1, 1.9 and 4.6 dB (2026-09-24). Against the 36 frozen capture files, twenty-seven agree with the unit inside half a decibel of level, and everything outside the three effect files is inside the measurement; `docs/fidelity_plan.md` has the table.

Against rgwan's 2026-09-22 take of the same demo with the effects and the filter stripped, the engine sits 2.8 dB under the unit in the median band, flat across the octaves: four songs at zero, Ana-Unison at -8. The Data List effects of 0.5.2 and earlier were the largest single number in the set at 17 to 29 dB over eighty-four segments. The filter and the effects compute, bit for bit, what the firmware's own DSP programs compute.

## KNOWN

- **Board.** SH7044 (HD64F7044F28, SH-2, 28 MHz, 256 KB internal flash) with a 2 MB M27C160 EPROM, 512 KB DRAM at 0x01000000, a 1 Mbit battery-backed NVRAM on CS1 (0x400000, user banks). Two YMP706-F tone generators at 0xC00000 and 0xC00400 (16 channels each, 8-bit write-only bus, PBUSY handshake). Two YSS236-F DSPs on CS2 with a 16-bit register block each, VOP3-2 (effects) at 0x800000 and VOP3-1 (the per-voice filter) at 0x800200, status at 0x800242. LED and LCD-contrast latch at 0x800100. Two Sanyo LC78834M 18-bit stereo DACs. Sources: the studiorepair.com FS1R gallery, rgwan's dumps, the firmware's address usage.
- **What is not on the bus.** 0x0140xxxx was listed for weeks as a second external device or NVRAM and is neither: the NVRAM is the 1 Mbit part on CS1, and 0x01000000-0x01FFFFFF is the SH7044's own DRAM area (rgwan, 2026-09-16). No code references the address, the EPROM words that read like pointers to it form no table, and anything that did reach it would alias onto 0x0100xxxx. `docs/research.md` 2.1.
- **Clocks and audio path** (rgwan, 2026-09-15, from the service manual and his board). One 24.576 MHz crystal runs the audio side: the main DAC is the I2S master at 512 Fs, exactly 48 kHz, and clocks the slave DAC, both VOP3s and both YMP706s. The CPU has its own 7 MHz crystal with the PLL at 4x, so the 28 MHz the SCI divisor implied is confirmed and the tick is not locked to the sample clock. FS1A's dry and send busses feed FS1B, which sums both chips and feeds VOP3-2; VOP3-2's SDO1 drives the main DAC and SDO3 the slave DAC, which is the INDIVIDUAL OUTPUT pair. VOP3-1 sits in a channel-level loop off both tone generators. The master volume is an analogue pot after the main DAC. Diagram and consequences in `docs/research.md` 2.0.1.
- **CS2 decode** (rgwan, 2026-09-16, from the schematic). Partial decoding on A8 and A9 alone: `VOP3_SELN = CS2N | A8`, `VOP3-1N = ~A9 | VOP3_SELN`, `VOP3-2N = A9 | VOP3_SELN`, `IC25_26_CLK = ~A8 | CS2N | WRL`. So VOP3-2 sits at 0x800000, VOP3-1 at 0x800200 and the 74HC374 pair at 0x800100, where IC26 drives LED0-7 off the low byte and IC25 drives CONTA/CONTB/CONTC off D8-10 into IC29, the LCD contrast DAC. Nothing above A9 is decoded, so each repeats every 0x400. IC31's WA/WD pins are open, so VOP3-1 has no external DRAM and cannot run a reverb. The firmware has a separate driver for each chip, `FUN_0000B5E2` and `FUN_00039648`; `docs/research.md` section 8.
- **The two DSPs.** The YSS236 ("VOP3") is Yamaha's programmable DSP; the FS1R has two, each with its own driver in the firmware: VOP3-1 the per-voice filter at 0x800200 and VOP3-2 the effects at 0x800000. The DSP itself, its instruction set and the programs the firmware uploads, is the separate [VOP3](https://github.com/musicastudio/VOP3) project; here are its hosts (`src/effects/host/`) and what the programs compute (`src/filters/`, `src/effects/`).
- **Engine, CPU side.** All 88 algorithms, the DX7 conversion, frequency words, EG rate and level conversion, velocity curves and attenuation, level key scaling, EG bias, note shifts, part detune, master tune, bend, the software pitch EG, LFO1 and LFO2, portamento, mono modes with priorities and legato, note and velocity limits, part volume/expression/balance, pan, performances, Fseq frame timing and playback. The 192.3 Hz tick (MTU2), the Fseq frame timer (CMT1), the YMP706 register map and every conversion table. See `docs/ymp706_registers.md`, and, since 2026-09-18, the voice image checked byte for byte against a running unit.
- **Controller sets.** The whole matrix, read out of FUN_00014DDC, FUN_00014AD0 and FUN_000191C0: the source bit order, the bipolar and raw source conversions, the clamped sum, the three scalers and which of the 48 destinations uses each, the per-part gate, and the destinations that are not a plain offset (pitch bias, amplitude EG bias, frequency bias, bandwidth, the LFO depths, Fseq speed, formant scratch). `docs/ymp706_registers.md` has the tables; `tools/ghidra_ctrl_dests.py` regenerates the handler decompilations.
- **Data.** Voice (608 bytes), performance (400), Fseq and system bulk layouts, the whole sysex parameter map, the 1408 preset voices, 384 performances and 90 Fseqs in the EPROM, the DX7 VCED and ACED maps.
- **The frequency EG.** A linear ramp at the amplitude EG's own rate ladder (`cal::FEG_TRAVERSE`), measured off `07_modulation_2`'s six `feg-*` segments (1 to 5 cents). It reaches every operator, fixed ones included: A123 Drum Kit 3's kick (all fixed operators, flat pitch EG) sweeps on WouterVanNifterick's unit, and rgwan's rig on 2026-10-25 tracked six fixed 1 kHz probes and a ratio one against the engine to within 1% at every point of the ramp. The 2026-09-25 exemption was a misread of `24_onset`, whose strikes after the first never re-key. `docs/findings.md` 2026-10-25
- **The channel allocator.** Round robin from the last channel allocated, and when nothing is free the steal ranks on the stored note from the part's own cursor, taking the first channel above the arriving note and otherwise the lowest note it passed. Read directly off the unit (`FS1R.unlock/captures/2026-09-25-7`, session6 `alloc`): notes 36..67 land on channels 1..31 then 0, a 33rd note of 100 takes channel 2 which held note 37, and the per-channel priority word at 0x010287DC is the note number exactly. Age is not consulted. `docs/findings.md` 2026-09-25
- **Effect parameter encoding.** Read back out of the preset performances and checked against every documented default: frequency index `20 * 2^(i/6)` Hz, gain `v - 64` dB, Q `v/10`, delay words in 0.1 ms, threshold `v - 127` dB, the XG LFO frequency and reverb time curves.

## INFERRED

- **YMP706 operator phase generator.** Measured 2026-10-18 on rgwan's unit, three takes off the S/PDIF tap: an operator's phase increment is an integer in units of 2^-23 of a cycle a sample, a frequency word's increment is `floor(2048 * 2^((w & 1023) / 1024))` shifted by `(w >> 10) - 21` with right shifts dropping bits, so a word plays `48000 * 2^(w / 1024 - 33)` Hz less the floor (word 26861 is 440.186 Hz). Fitted on one take and predicted blind on the next, twenty increments hit to 0.001 of a step, fixed and ratio-mode; the 2026-09-21 line at 23982 Hz agrees. Yamaha's US5744741 describes the same log/linear converter and shifter. The engine's operators run on it (`op_increment`, `cal.h`). Still inferred: a word with a fraction (FSVR's 14-bit bend, the frequency EG between steps), and whether any accumulator bits sit below 2^-23. `docs/findings.md` 2026-10-18
- **YMP706 EG shape and rate scaling.** Measured 2026-09-19 off four envelope recordings: the rate law, the 1.5 dB level step and the 1.5 dB carrier correction step all stand, and the 8-bit level register is 0.376287 dB, a halving every sixteen steps. The attack's shape, its floor, the hold's length and the key rate scaling were all wrong and are now measured. `docs/aeg.md` is the working. The key code law is a table measured at ten points three semitones apart plus one between them (key code 87, off the demo drum, 2026-09-24), interpolated truncating; the other two of every four key codes are interpolated, and the hold's fixed 8 ms lag has a six-millisecond scatter that is the 192.3 Hz tick rather than the chip. Register 0xC0 driven directly would read the rest, `FS1R.unlock/captures/fs1r_capture_session4.py`.
- **Modulation and sensitivity.** Per-op pitch and frequency modulation sensitivity scaling; feedback `0.5 * 2^(fb - 7)`. The modulation index is measured, 3.369 cycles at full level, off two sideband sweeps and confirmed by `03_fm`'s own spectra. Amplitude sensitivity is measured and disagrees with the model. Detune is settled and needs no constant at all: it is the EPROM's own key scaled `FRMDET`, measured over six octaves, and the engine reads the table rather than modelling it. See `docs/captures/capture_0918.md` and `docs/detune.md`.
- **The sum bus.** A read leaves it and t1 bit 1 of the algorithm word clears it, read off the connection table's pattern: the bit marks every place a second sum starts and none where it would drop an unread one, and that reading gives 65 and 80 the fan-out the Algorithm Sheet draws. No capture has checked it (`docs/ymp706_registers.md`, the algorithm word).
- **Frequency EG range.** The shape and the rate came off this list on 2026-09-25: the operator frequency EG is a linear ramp, not the exponential approach the engine had guessed, and its slope is the amplitude EG's own rate ladder read as a quarter tone per step rather than 0.375 dB (`cal::FEG_TRAVERSE` 24 semitones per traverse, two segments of `07_modulation_2` agreeing to 0.07 %). The filter EG on VOP3-1 is exponential and is a different block; the two do not behave alike. What is left here is the range, `FEG_SEMIS` 48 semitones for the full 128-step register swing: the segments read consistently with it but none of them pins the scale independently of `FEGLVL`. `docs/findings.md` 2026-09-25.
- **The formant window** (bandwidth and skirt) and the harmonic forms `all/odd/res`. Measured, 2026-09-19 to 2026-09-24: the window is a time (`docs/formant.md`), the skirt multiplies a `sin^p` exponent, and there are two window shapes, symmetric on the "2" forms and a `sin^p` rise on a `sin^2` fall on the "1" forms and the formant; all1 and all2 are that window alone, read by phase. All six harmonic forms sit inside 0.5 to 1.8 dB of the unit's line amplitudes at every skirt and `04_formant_2` at 0.44 dB of band shape. `docs/skirt.md`. Still inferred here: the two-period clamp on the formant's window (`FRMT_WL_MAX`), which no recorded note reaches. The noise formant chain came off this list on 2026-09-21 and was re-read on 2026-09-22: two digital one-poles with different coefficients, both measured against the register and the skirt on two files at three centres, the second pole's floor being what had read as a pedestal, and a two-sample mean on the unvoiced output; on 2026-09-24 the octave bands to 16 kHz agree inside 0.5 dB. The resonance carrier's level against the register is the one thing left in it, 2 to 3 dB between the two takes at settings 6 and 7. `docs/noise.md`.
- **The per-voice filter's response.** The CPU side is read from the firmware, including the cutoff and both resonance conversions, so what is left is how VOP3-1 reads a coefficient. Measured 2026-09-21 by `15_filter`, the first recording with a source that can see a filter: the corner is `17.4 * 2^(byte / 12)`, 0.098 octaves rms over thirteen points, where the one-pole reading of the coefficient had put byte 64 at 3751 Hz against the unit's 713. The resonance runs to a 19.9 dB peak where the engine topped out at 8.5, and the feedback goes as the cube of resonance table A. `RESO_COMP` is measured at zero rather than guessed. The three lowpasses are taps off one 4-pole ladder, and HPF, BPF and BEF a 2-pole state-variable filter, which is what the unit measures: 24, 18 and 12 dB per octave on the lowpasses and 12 on the HPF, to a decibel. `docs/filter.md`. Since 0.8.0 the filter computes what the firmware's own filter program computes (`src/filters/`), in place of this model.

## UNKNOWN

- **YMP706.** Which write starts a note (0xFC/FD is written before the registers load) and the exact response to every register above. No register documentation and no die shot; the chip is too rare for one. The pan path came off this list when FUN_00025BC8 and its siblings were read out, and the filter came off it for good: the firmware sends every filter parameter to VOP3-1 over the 0x800200 block, so this chip only switches the channel loop out to it. The operator phase generator came off it on 2026-10-18 (INFERRED above): measured off the audio, since the chip cannot be read on the bus.
- **Firmware odds and ends.** How the filter EG reaches the chip is settled (2026-09-23): the CPU steps it segment by segment through registers 0x2B to 0x2D and 0x21, on the VOP3-1 status interrupt, `FUN_0000D050` / `FUN_0000CB6C`. The 0x650000 access in the SCI transmit path is no longer on this list: rgwan confirmed it on hardware on 2026-09-19 as the NVRAM at offset 0x10000, aliased, since the 1 Mbit part decodes A0-A16 and 0x650000 minus 0x400000 is 0x250000, whose low 17 bits are 0x10000. A poke at 0x410000 reads back at 0x650000. It is the second user bank, not a device.
- **Hardware behaviour, chip side.** The CPU side of the note-on path is checked byte for byte, 240,476 checks over 802 segments with no mismatch, so what is left under this heading is the delay, modulation and insertion effects against a recording. The register sessions that settled the CPU side are indexed in [docs/captures/sessions.md](docs/captures/sessions.md); their data is in a private companion repo. rgwan owns the unit and the digital-out board. The tap is the main DAC's I2S input, after both effect DSPs and before the analogue volume pot, so a recording's absolute level is comparable and the effect blocks are always in the path.

## Open work

Grouped by what blocks each item rather than by subsystem. This file is the list of record. The maintainers mirror it on a private project board, which follows this file rather than the other way round, so a change belongs here first.

**Ready, needs no hardware**

- The delay, modulation and insertion effects against the effect recordings. The reverbs checked so far are within 2 to 12 LSB; the Data List models they replace read 17 to 29 dB over three files and eighty-four segments
- The effect programs in `src/effects/` named as the DSP blocks they are, as `aux_window.cpp` and `var_m07.cpp` are; `dsp_check` must stay ok
- The plug-in's macOS (universal) and Linux builds compile in CI, where `check_plugin` and `check_gui` pass on the arm64 macOS and x64 Linux legs; CI runs neither the Intel slice nor the plug-in inside a host. Windows is checked in the standalone, by `check_plugin` on x64 and x86, and by Steinberg's VST3 validator (47 of 47). `clap-validator` has not been run on any of them. On Linux the editor hands the keyboard back to the host's window after text entry, which no Linux host has confirmed yet, and a host that destroys the parent window before closing the editor would meet Xlib's default error handler
- The editor's own open items (names aren't editable, the LCD's performance menu lists the factory banks only) are in [docs/editor.md](docs/editor.md)
- CPU: measured by `bin/perfbench` on fixed scenarios from the factory banks, every optimisation gated by `tools/abcheck.py` to bit-identical renders (Tier A) or one 16-bit step (Tier B). On the dev box's P-core A020 Vox Morph at 8 notes is 16 % of the core at Full and 28 % with all 32 channels, Fat Line 9 %, a lone Zap! 5.5 %, after the 2026-10-28 round (the control refresh cached, the loop in segments, the filter bank and the tone generator on avx2 lanes; -17 to -37 % against the baseline, all Tier A). What is left: a channel pool across groups, and a float kernel on integer tables, which would be Tier B. Quality / Speed still takes played chords 23 to 32 % under Full. Polyphony (Options > Special, 2026-10-29: 32, 64, 96 or 128 voices, `docs/Differences.md`) leaves the 32-voice rows where they were and adds what more costs: 64 keys of Vox Morph on 128 channels 108 % of the P-core at Full, 32 keys of the four-part pad with 128 filter slots 77 %, one filtered note at 128 voices 6.6 % against 5.3 % at 32, since the filter banks past the second rest while they have nothing to do. Not a fidelity item; [docs/performance.md](docs/performance.md)
- Multicore: the channels' segments across threads, opt-in, the mix staying serial so the output is bit-identical at any thread count. Worth building now that 128 voices of a heavy patch read more than one P-core single-threaded (the row above); the design, the hazards read out of the code (one lane-kernel scratch, `busy[]`, per-thread FTZ, items that cannot be handed off) and the go/no-go are in [docs/performance.md](docs/performance.md), More than 32 voices

**Blocked on a hardware session** (`FS1R.unlock/captures/fs1r_capture_session4.py` carries all of it)

- The key code table's two unmeasured points in every four, and whether the chip latches a rate at a segment's start or re-reads the key code: register 0xC0 driven directly (`keycode`)
- The unvoiced resonance carrier's level against the bandwidth register, 2 to 3 dB between the two takes (`21_ures.mid`, in the `record` stage)
- Whether the chip latches per grain or slews the level register on a 3 to 5 ms constant. The lever is the grain rate
- The per-channel arrays at 0x0103B384 and 0x01044C, which the voice image does not carry
- Whether an EG hold delays the operator's key-on, its phase reset and frequency EG with it. The engine holds both still through the hold, which A043 To Warp's staggered holds over full-range sweeps argue for (`docs/findings.md` 2026-10-17). Two key-synced sines at 1:1, one with a hold of 40, at C2 and C4: a wave that changes between the notes means the reset waits for the hold, as here; the same wave at both means it runs from key-on
- odd1, odd2, res1 and res2 under a modulator at a normal pitch. They are read by phase with the modulation in the window and the carrier both, as Yamaha's patent builds them (US5610354, FIG. 11); held, they match `04_formant_3`, and at a fixed 0 Hz under a decaying modulator they match the unit to 1 dB (`docs/findings.md` 2026-10-23). The four at a normal pitch under a sine modulator, whose sidebands tell a window that takes the modulation from one that does not, are still to record
- A formant operator's Transpose (#31, v0.5.2): it now moves the operator's pitch, the grain rate, and leaves the formant's centre where Coarse and Fine put it. Checked by WouterVanNifterick against his own unit, not yet against a capture here. It moved `tools/regress.py`'s `ctrl-multi` (performance 28: centroid 171.2 to 167.5 Hz, peak count 4 to 5) against a v0.5.1 fingerprint; the other eighteen cases render as v0.5.1 does. A take of a formant operator swept through Transpose settles it

**Open research**

- Why one note took two parts in session 6. The unit sounded one note sent on one channel on **two** channels charged to two parts, with both parts' Rcv Max written as off, which neither a single channel nor a range produces. Parts 1 and 2 now hear Rcv Ch to Rcv Max as the owner's manual (p.25) and the Data List (2.2.3.2, 2.2.4) give it, each note bending by its own channel, so Preset C's guitar performances hear all six; the firmware's receive test itself has not been found. Session 6 now reads bytes 0x00/0x01/0x03/0x04 back per part and asserts one note takes one channel; the alternative is that 0x010289BC is not the channel-to-part map. `docs/findings.md` 2026-09-25

- Run the real firmware on an SH-2 core. `tools/ghidra_ctrl_dests.py` shows the pattern for reaching code Ghidra never turned into functions

**Parked**

- YMP706 emulation. Not possible without register semantics or a die, and the chip is too rare for a die shot. It stays a model
- Engine thread with a lookahead buffer, only if a host's buffer is ever the limit rather than the engine

**Reported**

A020 measures clean. Narrowed for B016: muting part-1 operators one at a time drops it 4.41x to **1.77x on operator 2 alone**, but op2's own envelopes are smooth across the step (its frequency EG is flat, so it never ticks). Unresolved and odd — B016's reverb, variation and insertion types all read 0, which is pass-through in both blocks, so either the type decode is wrong for this performance or a per-block level or pan word is being applied at the tick boundary rather than per sample. Further probing needs `Device`, not `Synth`, which renders silence on a `rom_perf` performance. **Deferred by James 2026-09-25: the effects are to be replaced by a VOP3 model, so this is not worth chasing in the current blocks.** The emulation replaced them in 0.8.0, so this wants a fresh measurement against it. The second bug the same capture exposed, `onset-feg`, is fixed. `docs/findings.md` 2026-09-25
